Artificial Neural Networks

Four short lessons, each with one idea and one thing you can play with. Work through them in order — each one answers a question the previous one opened.

The spine of the whole course: a neural network is a stack of linear transformations separated by non-linearities, trained by walking downhill on an error surface. Every design decision you will ever make — which activation, which optimizer, which regularizer, which callback — is an answer to a specific way that process fails.

Lesson 01 · ~15 min

Why a network needs a bend

Without a non-linearity, a hundred layers collapse into one and can only draw a straight line. Train a real network in the page and watch it happen. Plus: what the Universal Approximation Theorem actually says, and what your deck gets wrong about it.

→ You will be able to explain why depth is worthless without activations. Lesson 02 · ~15 min

Choosing the bend

Hidden layers and output layers pick activations for opposite reasons. Watch a gradient vanish through ten sigmoid layers, see why ReLU won, and learn the output-layer table that removes all guesswork from the last line of your model.

→ You will pick output activation + loss correctly, every time. Lesson 03 · ~15 min

Why so many optimizers

Gradient descent has exactly two bad habits. Momentum fixes one, adaptive rates fix the other, Adam does both. Race all four down the same ravine and find the learning rate where SGD explodes and Adam shrugs.

→ You will know which knob to turn when the loss misbehaves. Lesson 04 · ~20 min

The Keras control panel

Where your deck stops. Watch a network memorise noise in real time, then switch on L2, dropout and early stopping and watch the gap close. Ends with the table that turns a training curve into a diagnosis.

→ You will write a fit() call and justify every argument.

How to use these

Do not read them like slides. Each lesson has a quiz and a free-recall block at the end — answer out loud before revealing. Retrieving something from memory is what makes it stick; re-reading something familiar only makes it feel like it stuck. Come back in a few days and re-run the quizzes; the options reshuffle each time.

I am your teacher for this, not just the author of these pages. Ask me anything that does not land, ask for a worked example with real numbers, or ask for the next lesson when you are ready.
